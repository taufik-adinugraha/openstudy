B1 · The question

“Where does Indonesia sit in the global product space — and what did the nickel ban actually change?”

The story is a verified paradox, not a press release: after the 2020 raw-ore export ban, processed-nickel exports surged (USITC) — yet Indonesia’s economic complexity rank declined ~6 places over the decade to ≈ 69 (Harvard Atlas, 2023 data). The demo lets a client walk that tension themselves: what downstreaming bought, what basket concentration cost, and what the product space says is adjacent. Nothing sells analytical capability to a policy or corporate-strategy audience like resolving a story both sides tell one-sidedly.

B3 · Pipeline

Pipeline — annual DAG + quarterly check

Runs on a laptop or one CI job: DuckDB chews the full BACI corpus in minutes. Rebuilt when CEPII ships a new release (~each January); a quarterly cron only refreshes the Comtrade/BPS “latest pulse” panel.

ingest

Download BACI HS92 CSVs → DuckDB with schema checks (year coverage, row counts vs release notes, value totals vs prior release drift < 5%).

filter

Atlas-style sample: countries with population ≥ 1M and total trade ≥ US$1B (~130 countries); aggregate HS6 → HS4 (~1,200 products) for complexity; keep HS6 detail for the nickel value chain (2604 ore, 7202 ferroalloys, 7501–7508 nickel articles, 72xx stainless).

complexity

Per year 1995–2024: Balassa RCA → binary M matrix (RCA ≥ 1) → ECI/PCI via py-ecomplexity (eigenvector method), with the known PCI sign/scaling issue normalized against Atlas conventions; product proximity φ; Indonesia’s density and complexity-outlook gain per product.

layout

Product-space graph: maximum-spanning-tree backbone + edges φ ≥ 0.55 (~1,200 nodes, ~2,500 edges); force-directed layout computed once with fixed seed, coordinates cached as JSON — the browser never runs the simulation.

extracts

View models as parquet/JSON, each ≤ 3 MB: treemap shares per year, ECI-rank trajectories (Indonesia + peer set: Vietnam, Thailand, Malaysia, Philippines, India), nickel-chain value series, top-partner flows, product-space node states per year.

pulse

Quarterly: Comtrade + BPS latest-year panel refresh; reconciliation check runs here (G-B3).

brief

Insight brief from computed stats only, PR-reviewed — same mechanism as Flagship A.

publish

Static build, deploy, vintage stamp (“trade data through 2024 · pulse through Q2-2026”).

B4 · Validation gates

Validation gates

G-B1 — Atlas agreement. ECI country ranking: Spearman ρ ≥ 0.9 vs the Harvard Atlas for every overlap year, and Indonesia’s 2023 rank reproduced within ±3 places of ≈ 69. Divergences are diagnosed (sample filter, HS level, PCI sign) before any publish. Result (2026-08-29): PASS on rank — #71 of 158 vs Atlas #69 of 133, after adding the World Bank population ≥ 1M filter (the trade-floor-only sample gave #95 of 190 at the same percentile — a sample effect, diagnosed and fixed). Top-5 order (JPN, CHE, TWN, KOR, DEU) matches the Atlas. Spearman check pending an Atlas download.
G-B2 — Trajectory shape. Our series must reproduce the decade decline (~6 places) that frames the story; if our methodology shows otherwise, the story is rewritten to match our numbers — never the reverse. Result (2026-08-29): invoked. Our computation shows Indonesia flat in the mid-70s (2013 #80 → 2023 #71 → 2024 #78), not declining; peers climbed (VNM 84→57, PHL 53→37, IND 63→47) while processed-nickel exports rose 15× and ore fell to ~zero. Story rewritten: “more value, same place — while the neighbors climbed.”
G-B3 — BPS reconciliation. Indonesia total and nickel-chain export values agree with BPS within ~5%; the FOB/CIF and coverage differences that remain are footnoted with numbers, not waved at.
G-B4 — Story facts. Raw-ore collapse after 2020 and processed surge (ferronickel/stainless/NPI) must match the USITC working paper’s direction and rough magnitudes.
B5 · Dashboard

Dashboard — page anatomy

Interaction vocabulary from OEC / Atlas / Metroverse: treemap ↔ product space ↔ trajectory views over one dataset, every view URL-addressable, Grapher affordances on every chart.

  1. Hero — “Every product the world trades.” The product-space constellation assembles on a dark canvas: nodes stream in clustered by sector over ~4 s (fade-in under reduced motion), then Indonesia’s occupied products ignite in copper. One sentence sets the premise; scroll begins the story.
  2. Chapter 1 — “What Indonesia sells.” Morphing treemap 1995 → 2024: oil/gas and wood give way to palm, coal — then the nickel complex blooms. Year scrubber with animated share transitions.
  3. Chapter 1b — “Thirty years in thirty seconds.” BUILT Timelapse: bar-chart race of the top-10 export chapters 1995→latest with a HUD (year, goods exports, ECI rank, competitive-product count), play/pause, speed, scrubber; autoplays on scroll. The 3-D explorer gains ▶ to replay the constellation lighting up year by year.
  4. Chapter 2 — “The nickel gambit.” Scrollytold: 2014 first ban, 2020 full ban as annotated events; dual line chart — raw ore (collapsing) vs processed exports (surging); smelter map beat (Morowali, Weda Bay) that cross-links to Flagship A’s lights surge in the same district. The two flagships corroborating each other is the site’s best moment.
  5. Chapter 3 — “The complexity paradox.” ECI rank trajectory vs peers (Vietnam’s climb as the foil); the paradox stated plainly: more value, fewer places — because the basket concentrated. Product-space frames highlight what concentrated.
  6. Chapter 4 — “What’s adjacent.” Density × PCI scatter of Indonesia’s nearest unoccupied products — the forward-looking payoff a strategy client remembers.
  7. Explore — the instrument. Full 3-D product space (Three.js): hover → product name, PCI, world trade, Indonesia status; year slider re-lights the constellation; “altitude” toggle swaps depth for PCI; product search is hybrid — lexical over every HS6 description (prefix + one-edit fuzzy) layered with semantic matching via the lab’s local multilingual-E5 embedding service (Pustaka’s API), so Bahasa queries find English product names; degrades to lexical when the service is offline. Alternative noted for fully static deploys: in-browser encoder via transformers.js (30–100 MB first-visit download). Country switcher, treemap/partner tabs, full URL state remain roadmap.
  8. Brief + methodology. Generated-and-reviewed insight brief; methodology page with formulas (RCA, M, eigenvector ECI/PCI, φ), sample filters, goods-only scope, gates’ results, BACI/Comtrade/BPS attribution, downloads.

B6 · Design brief

Design brief

Identity: smelter metal — deep carbon ink, structural nickel-silver, molten-copper highlights. The constellation reads as cooling metal: quiet silver network, Indonesia’s products glowing hot.

ground #0C0F14 surface #161C25 text #E6E8EC nickel silver #AEB6C2 molten copper #D9722C verdigris (peers) #58A89B
  • Copper is Indonesia; verdigris is any peer country; nickel-silver is the world’s structure. Sector hues for product nodes: a 10-step desaturated categorical ramp tuned for the dark ground (validated per the dataviz palette method before build).
  • Type: shared lab system — Archivo, Public Sans, IBM Plex Mono, tabular-nums.
  • Motion: one assembly reveal; chapter transitions re-light rather than re-layout the constellation (position permanence keeps the mental map); reduced-motion swaps all of it for crossfades.
  • Light theme (fallback): paper ground, ink structure, same copper/verdigris semantics at AA-contrast values.

B8 · Risks & out of scope

Risks & out of scope

  • Methodology drift vs Atlas — Growth Lab occasionally revises methods; G-B1 pins us to observable agreement, and any residual divergence is documented rather than hidden.
  • py-ecomplexity PCI sign issue — known (repo issue #1); normalization against Atlas conventions is an explicit pipeline step with a test.
  • 2024-vintage honesty — BACI trails ~2 years; the Comtrade/BPS pulse panel exists precisely so the page never looks stale, but the two data planes are never mixed in one chart.
  • Out of scope: services trade, subnational trade, tariff simulation, forecasting, ask-the-data Q&A (phase 2).
