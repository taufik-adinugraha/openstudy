Jakarta Is Sinking

Sentinel-1 InSAR land subsidence for Greater Jakarta, fused with elevation, population, and observed floods into a kelurahan-level exposure map — and a clock. Built first on an openly deposited velocity field, then validated against our own InSAR run.

C1 · The question

“How fast is the ground under Jakarta sinking — and who is standing on it?”

North Jakarta is among the fastest-subsiding urban land on Earth: coastal hotspots drop 3–5 cm every year, and single points have exceeded 10. Radar interferometry sees it from orbit to millimetre precision. This case turns that measurement into the thing a city, an insurer, or a utility actually needs: which kelurahan, how many people, how many years. The decisions behind it — D17 reuse a deposited field first and run our own InSAR as a gate, D18 full flood-exposure scope, D19 open 30 m elevation now — trade a little fidelity for zero registrations and a day-one map.

C3 · Pipeline

Pipeline — static rebuild

ingest

Range-fetch the Jakarta slice of the Zenodo velocity CSV (lon 106.55–107.15, lat −6.55…−5.95), GNSS records, GLO-30 tiles, WorldPop + GHSL, kelurahan + BPBD flood polygons (paginated ArcGIS REST), 2020 flood extents. All small; ~300 MB total.

grid

Average duplicate coordinates (overlapping frames), mask points with reported sd > 1 cm/yr, grid to 0.001° EPSG:4326 (~111 m — matches LiCSAR pixel spacing so G-C3 compares like with like; UTM 48S is used only for polygon area math downstream), write tiled GeoTIFF with overviews. Hotspots are characterised by the neighbourhood median and the fastest decile (p10) — the p10 reads a hotspot's core the way the literature's peak values do, while area medians under-read it; both are printed everywhere. Sign convention: negative = subsidence, stated everywhere.

insar

LiCSBAS on 098A 2017–2024 clipped to 106.6/107.1/−6.45/−6.0: steps 01 (GeoTIFFs + GACOS) → 02 → 03op GACOS → 05op clip → 11–16 (network, inversion, velocity, filtering) → LiCSBAS_cum2vel; LOS → vertical using geo.E/N/U; referenced to a stable central pixel near CJKT. ~13 GB download, tens of minutes compute. Runs after the nightlights backfill releases bandwidth.

fuse

One common ~100 m analysis grid (WorldPop’s own cells, EPSG:4326); kelurahan burned by cell centre so city totals are conserved. Per kelurahan: median and p10 subsidence over covered cells, population (WorldPop; GHS-POP as cross-check), built-up surface (GHS-BUILT-S), share of ground below +1 m / +2 m — “ground” per cell is the minimum of its GLO-30 pixels, since GLO-30 is a surface model and the lowest 30 m pixel in an urban cell is a road or yard, not a roof — flood-history frequency, RT count and max depth (BPBD 2021–24, distinct event dates), and Jan 2020 flood share (UNOSAT ∪ EOS). The clock: years until a kelurahan’s low ground (p10 of the ground proxy) falls below +1 m / mean sea level at its median and fastest-decile rates, plus people/built-up below each threshold per year 2025→2050 — stated as a linear extrapolation with its assumptions (rates held, uncovered cells still, DSM epoch 2011–2015 taken as today), never as a prediction.

validate

Gates G-C1…G-C5 (below); results to stats.json for the methodology page.

export

NaN-safe JSON view-models: simplified kelurahan GeoJSON + exposure table with 2025→2050 series; the velocity field as a small value-encoded PNG coloured client-side (hero animation + map overlay — PMTiles dropped, the 600×600 grid doesn’t need tiles and static hosting stays gate 8), plus a coloured overlay for MapLibre; GNSS stations with measured-vs-InSAR; hero render. Hotspot time-series arrives with G-C3 (LiCSBAS cumulative displacement).

C4 · Validation gates

Validation gates

G-C1 — Literature agreement. In the NW-coast hotspots (Muara Baru, Kamal Muara, Kosambi, PIK) the fastest decile (p10) subsides ≥ 2 cm/yr (and ≤ 12) with the neighbourhood median ≥ 1 cm/yr; central Jakarta (Monas, Ancol) stays within ±1 cm/yr of zero. Point-based PS studies report higher peaks (Penjaringan up to 10 cm/yr) — a scale difference stated on the page, not a discrepancy hidden. Result 2026-08-30: PASS (Muara Baru −4.15 | −4.92, Kosambi −4.36 | −5.18, Monas +0.16 | −0.21 cm/yr, median | p10).
G-C2 — GNSS agreement. InSAR vertical rate at each Jakarta GNSS station (CJKT, CTGR, CBTU) within ±5 mm/yr of the published GNSS rate, after frame differences (IGS14 vs local) are reconciled and documented. Result: PASS (CJKT −4.6 vs −6.4, CTGR −1.4 vs −2.9, CBTU +1.9 vs −0.5 mm/yr).
G-C3 — Our run vs the deposit (D17). LiCSBAS vertical velocity vs the deposited field over Jakarta: pixelwise correlation ≥ 0.7 and hotspot medians within 1 cm/yr. Divergence is diagnosed (reference point, atmospheric correction, unwrapping errors) before publish. Result: pending — deferred until the 13 GB LiCSAR download has bandwidth.
G-C4 — Exposure sanity. Kelurahan count is 267; the WorldPop total over the kelurahan lands within ±15 % of the census (BPS SP2020: 10,562,088); GHS-POP within ±25 % of WorldPop; ≥ 90 % of mainland kelurahan carry ≥ 30 % InSAR coverage; no NaNs, no zero-population mainland kelurahan. Result: PASS (WorldPop 10,724,798, +1.5 %; GHSL −2.1 %; coverage 100 %).
G-C5 — Flood plausibility (v1’s G-C4, renumbered when G-C4 became the sanity gate). Kelurahan exposure ranking correlates with observed BPBD flood frequency 2021–24 (Spearman ≥ 0.5) and the 2020 extent overlaps the top-exposure kelurahan disproportionately. Floods have many causes; the gate tests plausibility, not causation. Result: FAIL, published red with its diagnosis — ρ = 0.16: the BPBD 2021–24 record is dominated by riverine floods along the Ciliwung in higher-ground East Jakarta (flood frequency is inversely related to low ground, ρ = −0.14), and RT-level reporting under-captures North-Jakarta tidal flooding. The disproportionality half holds: the Jan 2020 extent covers 12 % of the 20 most-exposed kelurahan vs 5 % city-wide.
C5 · Dashboard

Dashboard — page anatomy

  1. Hero — “The ground is moving.” Signature moment: Jakarta rendered in the bathymetric ramp, dark and still; then the velocity field settles — hotspots sink into deep cyan over ~3 s while a clock counts the centimetres lost since 2017. Reduced motion: static.
  2. Chapter 1 — “Where it sinks fastest.” Ranked hotspot list with rates, measurement window, and the literature/GNSS checks alongside — the trust move, up front.
  3. Chapter 2 — “Why.” Groundwater extraction, sediment compaction, building load — cited narrative, no new data claims.
  4. Chapter 3 — “Who stands on it.” Population exposure by kelurahan; the clock per kelurahan; the two population sources shown side by side.
  5. Chapter 4 — “The floods already came.” January 2020 extent and 2021–24 flood history over the subsidence field; the plausibility gate shown as a chart.
  6. Explore. MapLibre with a full 2-D canvas fallback (point-in-polygon hit testing) so a browser with WebGL disabled loses nothing: metrics (fastest decile, median, flood events, low ground) + the velocity-field overlay, GNSS/hotspot labels; kelurahan click → panel (rate, p10, population, built-up, low ground, flood count, the clock, 2025→2050 sparkline); the clock’s year slider recolours exposure; URL state. Hotspot time series arrives with G-C3. deck.gl not needed.
  7. Methodology. Both velocity fields explained; LOS→vertical; frames and windows; gates’ results; every licence and attribution; the clock’s assumptions in plain words.
C6 · Design brief

Design brief

Identity: water and depth. The ramp reads like a bathymetric chart — stable ground in deep navy, fast subsidence in luminous cyan — and coral is reserved for flood exposure so “sinking” and “flooded” never share a hue.

ground #070D16 surface #0E1926 text #E4EBF2 ramp stable #0A1F3A ramp fast / accent #35C6E0 flood coral #F26D5B
  • Type: shared lab system. Numbers always carry units and windows: “−4.1 cm/yr · 2017–2023”.
  • Motion: the hero’s settling animation is the one orchestrated moment; everything else is 200–300 ms micro-interaction.
  • Every chart interactive (D9): hover inspection, linked kelurahan highlighting between map and lists, URL-addressable views.

C8 · Risks & out of scope

Risks & out of scope

  • Jakarta Satu licence — no explicit terms; used with attribution and the HDX COD-AB fallback for polygons. If challenged, the BPBD flood-history layer is replaced by the UNOSAT/EOS extents only.
  • Scale mismatch — SBAS at 75–100 m vs PS points: hotspots read lower than headline studies; disclosed in chapter 1.
  • LOS → vertical — single-geometry conversion assumes negligible horizontal motion; the descending frame enables 2-D decomposition for 2017–2022 if needed.
  • The clock is an extrapolation — linear rates, no groundwater-policy scenarios; labeled as such.
  • Out of scope: sea-level-rise projections (the Zenodo RSL product exists; phase 3 at most), building-level damage, real-time monitoring.
